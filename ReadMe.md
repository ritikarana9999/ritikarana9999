I sit at the point where a stakeholder says *"can we understand why this is happening?"* and I actually go find out — with SQL, a notebook, and occasionally a model I probably didn't need to build but built anyway.

## 🧭 What I do

My day job is asking "so what does the business actually want to know?" and turning that into something measurable — churn analysis, customer segmentation, reporting that people trust enough to act on. The technical side isn't a hobby bolted onto the job; it's how I get to the answer faster than a spreadsheet would let me.

That means:
- Translating vague stakeholder asks into a data problem with a testable answer
- Building the model or dashboard myself instead of just spec'ing it for someone else
- Caring as much about *"will anyone actually use this insight"* as about model accuracy

## 🧠 How I think about problems

I don't trust a metric until I know what decision it's supposed to change. A lot of "data projects" are really business questions wearing a lab coat — the model matters less than whether the question was the right one to ask in the first place.

So before I open a notebook, I try to answer:
- What decision does this actually inform, and who's making it?
- What's the simplest thing that answers the question — does it need to be a model, or just a well-chosen chart?
- If I'm wrong, how would I know?

The fun part is that the answer is sometimes "yes, this genuinely needs a vision transformer," and sometimes it's "no, you need three columns in Power BI." Knowing which is which is the actual skill.
